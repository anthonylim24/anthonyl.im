# Lim (engineering reference)

Read before changing `frontend/src/chat/`, `server/src/lim/` or `POST /api/invoke`. Design context (personality, look, behaviours): [`PRODUCT.md`](../PRODUCT.md).

Code: `frontend/src/App.tsx` (page shell), `frontend/src/chat/` (`chatMachine.ts` the turn as a pure reducer, `useChat.ts` the shell that runs it, `conversation.ts` message shapes + storage + history, `Transcript.tsx` memoised messages + follow-ups, `Composer.tsx` the message box, `suggestions.ts`, `LimStage.tsx` lazy 3D stage, `LimArt.tsx` SVG Lim, `caret.ts`, `eggs.ts` typed easter eggs, `world/` items and places (spec, catalog, client API), `sound.ts` WebAudio foley, `chat.css` tokens + code theme, `scene/limScene.ts` behaviour, `scene/limLook.ts` palette + expressions + TSL materials, `scene/fx.ts` particle pools + the tuft, `scene/items.ts` item meshes, `scene/toyMesh.ts` + `toys/*.mesh` the catalog's stage models, `scene/fixtures.ts` hoop and goal, `scene/thumbs.ts` live pictures of made toys, `toyArt.ts` + `toys/` the catalog's pre-renders, `scene/env.ts` sky and weather, `scene/scenery.ts` landscapes), `frontend/src/styles/chatbot.stylex.ts`, `messageContent.stylex.ts`, `components/message-content.tsx`.

## The chat

- **One turn is a state machine** (`chat/chatMachine.ts`, tested in `chat/__tests__/chatMachine.test.ts`): `idle`, `intercepting` (the toy-world shortcut is deciding), `waiting` (asked, nothing shown), `undecided` (what arrived, "@" or an opening fence, could still be a world order), `streaming`, `ordering` (a `@@world` order is being written; held back and handed over whole). Events: `SEND`, `INTERCEPT_HIT` / `INTERCEPT_MISS`, `CHUNK`, `STREAM_END`, `ORDER_RESULT`, `FAIL`, `STOP`, `RESET`, `CALM`. Every event names its reply, so a request that no longer owns the chat (Stop, New chat, a newer send) cannot change it. `useChat` does the work and reports it; Lim's mood (`moodFor`) and the "making" lines (`makingLine`) are derived from the phase and one `lastOutcome`, never stored.
- **A reply's text is only what Lim wrote.** Status is `streaming | done | stopped | error` and an error carries its own `error` line, shown under any partial text with Try again. Older saves that appended `⚠️ …` to the text are split when read (`readStored`).
- **Failures speak in Lim's voice** (`LIM_ERRORS` in `effect/chatErrors.ts`): 400 / 413 too long, 429 a breather, 5xx wobbly, an SSE `{"error"}` event part-way (`ReplyStreamError` reason `server`), a stream that ended without `[DONE]` (`cut`), no connection (`offline`), and the 45 s watchdog (`TimeoutError`). Each keeps what had arrived in `partialContent`. Only JSON string chunks are text; any other payload is dropped (`readInvokeChunk`).
- **History fits the server's limits** (`server/src/routes/invoke.ts`: 8,000 characters per message, 40 messages). `toHistory` sends at most 30 turns and clamps each with `clampTurn` (a question keeps its start, a reply its end); the composer has `maxLength` 8,000 and counts down from 7,000.
- **Persistence:** saved when a turn starts or settles, and on `pagehide` / hidden tab, with a reply still streaming written as stopped. Not on every chunk.
- **Rendering:** `Transcript`, each message and `MessageContent` are memoised, and the composer owns its text, so a keystroke or a chunk re-renders only what changed. Model-written markdown images render as links, never `<img>` (no fetches to hosts the model names).
- **`/?q=…`** prefills the composer (trimmed, capped, never sent by itself) and is removed from the address bar. After a settled reply, up to three opening questions not yet asked appear as follow-ups.
- **Shortcuts:** Esc stops a reply; Alt+Shift+S night, Alt+Shift+M sound (a modifier, so typing never triggers them; listed in the hint row and on the buttons' `aria-keyshortcuts`).

## Lim's world (items and places)

a visitor can give Lim things and send it places, from the toy box (the brick button in the composer) or by asking in chat. Code: `chat/world/spec.ts` (specs, cleaners for untrusted JSON, the catalog, the model-free matcher), `chat/world/worldApi.ts` (reading the model's orders out of the stream + persistence), `scene/items.ts` (procedural meshes and the two looks a toy can wear), `scene/toyMesh.ts` (the catalog's Blender models on the stage), `scene/fixtures.ts` (the hoop and the goal), `scene/thumbs.ts` (toy-box pictures), `scene/env.ts` (sky backdrop, weather), `scene/scenery.ts` (the landscape behind the stage), `server/src/lim/world.ts` (the model's instructions).
- **One spec format.** An item is 1 to 6 primitive parts (ball, box, cylinder, cone, disc, drop, star, heart, bean, ring) plus traits; a place is a palette, horizon, sun / moon / planet, stars, weather, gravity, grip and a mood; a tweak changes the air (weather, gravity, grip, light) without going anywhere. A part may carry a `paint` (seams, patches, stripes, spots) in an `ink` colour, and a place lists its `scenery` (trees, pines, palms, a volcano, clouds, coral, fish, buildings and so on; `sceneryFor` picks some for a place that names none). Catalog entries (44 items, 8 places) and things the model invents are the same data, drawn by the same gel shader and ink outline. Add catalog entries in `spec.ts` and mirror the id in `server/src/lim/world.ts` (`server/src/lim/world.test.ts` checks parity).
- **Who decides: the chat model.** No separate call decides whether a message is a world order (Jev, below, only picks which Gemini model answers). When the 3D scene is up, the page sends the world (item names, place, weather) with every question to `POST /api/invoke`, whose system prompt gains `worldPrompt()` (`server/src/lim/world.ts`). The model answers normally, or, if and only if the message is an order for the toy world, with one line: `@@world {"ops":[...],"reply":"..."}`. `useChat` watches the stream for that marker, never shows it, and hands the finished line to `onWorld` (`App.tsx`), which cleans and applies the ops and shows Lim's line instead. So a real question is never delayed or misread by a pattern list, and loosely phrased orders ("I want a dragon", "kick it") work. In front of the model sits one shortcut, `localIntent` in `spec.ts`: an order whose whole text parses and names something Lim already knows (catalog, made earlier, or in the world) is acted out at once with no model call. It must stay strict: `chat/__tests__/world.test.ts` holds some 220 strings it may never act on. After editing `worldPrompt()` or changing the model, run `bun scripts/eval-lim-world.ts [model]` (real API, not CI): 127 cases judged through the page's own reader and cleaners: chat messages that must be answered in prose, orders that must come back as `@@world` with the right op, held-out sets of each that appear nowhere in the prompt, a conversation with earlier orders in it, and a page with no 3D scene. It costs real tokens. Last full runs: Neon 127/127 (November 2026; median reply about 5 s); Hydrogen 127/127 (November 2026, with the sports rule, part paint and place scenery in the prompt). Re-run both before relying on a prompt change. World orders go back to the model as history in their `@@world` form (`ChatMessage.sent`), and toy-box replies the model never wrote are left out. Without a live 3D scene the page sends `world.unavailable` and the model is told the toy box is down. Both the chat and the orders run on Gemini, with Groq as the backup. Before the call, Jev (TypeSafe, `server/src/lim/jev.ts`) asks one Choice question of the message, recent turns and the world: regular chat goes to Gemini 4 Hydrogen, play with the jelly (orders and the toys Lim invents) to Gemini 4 Neon. The route only picks the model; both get the same prompt, so a misroute still answers correctly. No live scene skips Jev, and no key, an error or a 2 s timeout falls back to Hydrogen. `GEMINI_MODEL` does not change Lim's models. The server logs the model and route for each message (`[invoke] gemini-4-neon (route=jelly via=jev pJelly=0.93)`). With `JEV_API_KEY` set and no model argument, the eval routes each case through Jev and reports routing accuracy too; `--route-only` scores Jev alone with no Gemini calls (cheap: run it after editing `INTENT_QUESTION`). Last route-only runs: 121–122/124, median ~85 ms, no misses in the held-out sets; the misses send toy orders to Hydrogen ("Lim deserves a medal"), which handles them. Writing an invention takes seconds, so that wait is a performance: `scene.conjure(true)` gathers a ring of sparkles over a muttering Lim and the reply cycles progress lines (`MAKING` in `App.tsx`); a failed order fizzles and Lim says so. With motion reduced, items simply appear and actions apply their outcome only.
- **Never trust a spec.** Everything from the model or from localStorage passes `cleanItem` / `cleanPlace` / `cleanTweak` / `cleanOps`: enums checked, numbers clamped, part and op counts bounded, names stripped to plain words, colours pulled into the palette, item size normalised. Nothing from the model is used before it is cleaned.
- **What Lim does with things.** Every item: look, kick, hug, put away. By trait: bouncy / floaty → keepy-uppy; edible → eat (hot and cold have their own reactions); hat / glasses → wear (sprung to the head, third poke knocks them off); soft / cozy → nap; musical → dance; scary → flee; prickly → ouch. Lim also plays with items unprompted and reacts to the place's mood. Six items at most; the oldest makes way.
- **Sports.** `hoop` and `goal` are fixtures, not toys (`scene/fixtures.ts`): one of each at most, standing at the right and left edges, outside the six-item limit, built from static colliders (backboard, rim, pole; posts, bar, nets). While one is up the camera pulls back to show the court (`court` in `layout`). A ball that was thrown, kicked or dropped scores when it crosses the rim plane downward or the goal line (`Fixture.scored`): 2 for the hoop, 3 from across the stage, 1 for a goal, credited to whoever sent it last (`toy.by`). The tally shows in a pill on the stage (`LimStage`), is announced to screen readers, and is saved with the world. `shoot` has Lim line up and take three shots (about 6 in 10 go in); "play basketball" / "play soccer" set up whatever is missing (`SPORT` in `spec.ts`). Left alone with a ball and a hoop, Lim practises.
- **Toy box.** Each toy is a card with its picture on top and its name below (`ToyCard.tsx`). Catalog toys are Blender pre-renders (`chat/toys/<key>.webp`, keyed in `chat/toyArt.ts`; how to re-render: [`docs/art.md`](art.md)): a still, and on hover or keyboard focus one turn played at 30 fps from a 48-frame strip (`toyTurn` in `chatbot.stylex.ts`, CSS `steps()`), loaded the first time it is wanted and skipped under reduced motion. A new catalog toy needs a model in `art/toys.py` and a render, or `chat/__tests__/ToyCard.test.tsx` fails. Toys the model made have no pre-render: `scene/thumbs.ts` draws them with one small renderer, made only once the box holds one; without a GPU they show a colour swatch.
- **Toys on the stage.** A catalog toy on the stage wears the same Blender model as its picture, in the stage's own gel shader and ink outline (`modelLook` in `scene/items.ts`); the hoop and the goal keep `scene/fixtures.ts`, which is drawn to fit their colliders. The physics does not change. The toy is still a `Jelly` built from its spec, and the model rides it (`scene/toyMesh.ts`). The model is fitted over the item's parts and its geometry never changes: each frame it is carried by one matrix (`carryMatrix`), the body's turn, squash and stretch with 70% of the particles' own fit blended in (`Jelly.fit`, so a landing still squishes it), lifted whole if a corner would dip through the floor. The GPU applies it, so a model costs the same per frame however detailed it is, and every toy of a kind shares one geometry (`ItemMaterials.model`). Each colour group gets the gel material (the spec's paint on the body colour), near-black groups are flat ink, and the hull's width is per vertex so thin shells get a finer line. Groups the exporter marks as glass (bit 24 of the colour word: the snow globe's dome, the crystal, the ice cube) are blended glass in the lit look (`glassMaterial`): thin face-on, denser at the rim, drawn after the transparent floor, writing no depth, and left out of the ink hull, whose far side would show through. Transmission was tried first and refracts nothing, because the floor and sky are transparent and transmission sees only opaque surfaces. Models are `chat/toys/<key>.mesh` (11 to 28 kB, made by `bun art/render.ts meshes`, [`docs/art.md`](art.md)). The starter toys' models download while the GPU starts; once Lim has entered, the rest are fetched and shaped one at a time while the page is idle (`prefetchModels`, skipped under Save-Data), so a toy arrives as itself. Until its model arrives, or if it fails, a toy wears its jelly look (`jellyLook`), and the swap waits for `renderer.compileAsync`. Only a catalog spec gets a model (`catalogKey` compares name, size and parts after `cleanItem`, so a saved world still matches); a toy the model made, or a catalog toy it recoloured or resized, stays jelly. In hand a model keeps its depth test and is drawn near the camera instead (`nearDepth` in `limLook.ts`), because unlike the jelly body it overlaps itself. Tests: `chat/__tests__/toyMesh.test.ts`.
- **Shared materials, built before Lim enters.** Every toy, hoop and goal draws from one set of materials (`createItemMaterials` in `scene/items.ts`): one shader per kind of surface (a fill per paint pattern, soft or rigid; glass; ink; the outlines; a net). A toy's colours and an outline's width are data on a cheap material instance (`userData`, read per draw by `onObjectUpdate` uniforms), and three.js keys a shader build by its nodes, so a new toy compiles nothing. `compileAsync` builds only the main pass, so while the loader shows, `warm` in `limScene.ts` draws one probe per shader behind the camera through the real post and shadow passes, with everything hidden made visible, in a few batches. Between batches it runs a few real frames (`rehearse`, not counted as the first frame), so the entrance is not the first time the simulation runs: cold, Lim's first step cost about 30 ms at 4× and its type feedback for the toolkit's `Jelly` was thrown away when the scene first wrote fractional sizes, which a spare body could not reproduce. Measured with `frontend/scripts/lim-perf.mjs` (a fresh browser and no shader cache each run, 4× CPU throttle, High, twelve toys spawned 0.7 s apart): main thread blocked 2,560 to 2,690 ms before, 0 after; frames over 50 ms about 45 before, 5 to 8 after; p99 frame 217 to 250 ms before, 50 to 67 after. Boot (the 4 s from the entrance) went from 1 or 2 long tasks and a 49 ms simulate step to none and 2.5 ms. At 1× every phase holds 16.7 ms frames and a toy's first draw costs at most 5 ms.
- **Quirks.** The twenty newest catalog toys each have a quirk (`scene/quirks.ts`, the `QUIRKS` registry keyed by catalog key): the rocket lifts off on a spark trail, the music box lulls Lim to sleep, the spinning top makes it dizzy, the magnet pulls toys in, the lantern floats and lights the floor. A quirk answers a tap, a knock (Lim, or another toy, with the impulse), Lim eating it, or the frame, and is built only from the stage's pieces handed to it as a `Stage` (sparkles, flakes, bubbles, dust, a floor glow, Lim's faces and moves), so it plays the same in both looks. Any toy, invented ones too, also answers by its traits (`tapTraits`, `tickTraits`: a musical toy plays a note, a glowing one lights the floor), and two toys knocked together hard clink. A quirk's timers live in the toy's `memo` and go with it. Under reduced motion the scene is a still picture that takes no taps, so no quirk runs. Tests: `chat/__tests__/quirks.test.ts` (every quirk belongs to a catalog toy, and the rocket, magnet, music box, ice cream, spinning top and glow behave).
- **Landscapes.** `scene/scenery.ts` stands instanced, hand-built props behind the stage where the floor runs out at the backdrop's horizon: one draw call per kind, pop-up-book entrances, wind sway, drifting clouds and fish, lava and smoke, lit windows, distance haze, an ink hull on the near props. Per-vertex and per-instance values are interleaved into one buffer each, because WebGPU allows eight vertex buffers a draw. `SceneryStage.thin()` halves the instances when the stage reports a slow GPU. Check a change with `cd frontend && node scripts/capture-lim.mjs <out-dir>` (court, toy box, every place; needs Vite on :5173).
- The world (items, place, tweak, score) persists in `localStorage` under `lim-world-v1`; things the model made are kept under `lim-made-v1` and listed in the toy box so they can be had again for free.

## Scene and runtime notes

- Theme classes: `chatbot-shadow` (day) / `chatbot-dark` (night), both defined in `frontend/src/chat/chat.css` (`--chat-*` vars), not `index.css`
- `html:has(.chatbot-*)` paints the canvas so iOS safe areas blend
- The 3D scene is lazy (`import('./scene/limScene')`); `LimArt` SVG is the first paint and the no-WebGPU/WebGL fallback
- `LimStage` is a union: `drawing` (chunk loading), `loading` (GPU init), `ready`, `live` (first frame), `failed` (`unsupported`, `init`, `lost`). A lost GPU (`device.lost` on WebGPU, `webglcontextlost` on WebGL2, via `Stage.onLost` in `three/stage.ts`) disposes the scene and shows the drawing for good. Whenever the scene goes, `onWorld(null)` tells the page, so the toy box says it is unavailable instead of offering cards that do nothing
- **Loading screen** (`chat/LimLoader.tsx`, machine `chat/loader.ts`, styles `styles/limLoader.stylex.ts`). `LimStage` reports `onLoad(StageLoad)`: `loading` with a `step` (`code`, `gpu`, `toys`, `light`, `warm`) and `done` (0 to 1), then `ready` or `failed`; the page passes back `revealed`, and Lim makes its entrance only once that is true. The reducer runs `loading → leaving → gone`, or `loading → chat-first → gone` when the visitor picks Chat first (the stage keeps loading behind the chat; a pill on the stage shows the count, then cheers when it arrives). `revealed` turns true as the exit starts, so the live Lim is mid-pop as the sheet uncovers it. `failed` goes straight to `gone`; a load under `QUICK_MS` gets a short fade. It shows once per page visit (`loaderSeen` in `App.tsx`), never again on a rebuild. The page behind is `inert` while it is up, and focus returns to the composer (desk) or the conversation. Every animation is transform or opacity on the compositor, because shader compiles hold the main thread for hundreds of ms on phones; reduced motion drops the bounce, wobble and leap for a fade. Since `enter()` runs as the exit starts, the scene's render loop and its idle catalog-model prefetch share the main thread with the falling sheet; the compositor-only exit is what keeps it smooth. Tests: `chat/__tests__/loader.test.ts`, `chat/__tests__/LimLoader.test.tsx`, `__tests__/App.loader.test.tsx`
- **Graphics: Auto, High, Low** (the sparkles button in the header; `scene/quality.ts`, kept under `lim-graphics`). They resolve to a tier: the toon look (the unlit gel, also Low) or the lit look, which matches the Blender renders (`scene/lighting.ts`: the studio HDR, a warm key that casts the only shadow onto a catcher over the floor, front, fill and rim; `plastic` in `limLook.ts`: clear-coated sorbet plastic, tone mapped per material so the unlit sky, floor and ink keep their colours). The lit look runs one of four post rungs (`scene/post.ts`): `lit` (MSAA and bloom), `ao` (+ GTAO with TRAA), `ssr` (+ screen-space reflections), `gi` (SSGI for AO and one bounce of colour). Only surfaces write the normal, velocity and material targets; transparent sheets write nothing there, and unlit materials give the bounce no albedo. The canvas is transparent over the page's sky, so TRAA's colour is clamped to the frame's alpha (WebKit smears a premultiplied pixel brighter than its alpha into a line along the row). The look is fixed when the scene is built (changing it rebuilds the scene); the rung changes live. High is `gi`; Auto starts at `gi` on a desktop and `ssr` on a phone, or where it settled on this device (`lim-graphics-fit`: a lit rung for a week, the toon look for a day). WebGL2 and reduced motion get the lit look at `lit`. A WebGL2 phone gets no shadow map and keeps full-strength blob shadows instead: the iOS Simulator's WebGL2 fails the shadow test everywhere, greying the floor, and no real phone without WebGPU was at hand to show otherwise. While frames fall behind, Auto drops GI, then reflections, then AO, then pixel ratio, and gives up the lit look only at the lowest pixel ratio over 30 ms a frame. It acts on a second slow window in a row, at once over 60 ms (straight to `lit`), never in the first 5 s, and only when the stage's own work (CPU, or GPU from `onSubmittedWorkDone`) cannot fit the frame, so a browser capping frames at 30 Hz is not mistaken for a slow device. Measured GPU time per frame (M1 Max, 1477×1575): toon 2.8, `lit` 3.8, `ao` 7.1, `ssr` 8.6, `gi` 9.7 ms. Tests: `chat/__tests__/quality.test.ts`.
- Reduced motion freezes the jiggle and renders on demand; CSS beats are paused; stunts and easter eggs become a still smile
- Settled toys sleep (no sim, no geometry sync) until something touches them; `Jelly.step` allocates nothing
- Physics is in-house (`three/jelly.ts` + `three/physics.ts`): shape matching in small steps against signed-distance colliders (sphere, capsule, rounded box, torus) with friction and restitution per surface, a rigid core sphere and adaptive substeps (`core`, `maxTravel`) so nothing fast tunnels through a rim or a post, and `bodyImpulse` for body-to-body bounce, rub and spin
- Bodies meet where their surfaces do. A toy collides as the box its parts fill (what its model is fitted to), rounded toward an ellipsoid by its shape (`BodyShape`, `ROUND` in `limScene.ts`: a ball is round, a box keeps its corners) and carried by its body's turn and squash; `reachAlong` measures each along the line between them, so a rocket meets you nose first or side on. Lim stays a low, wide ball for its skirt. The dent, the clink and the sparkle go where the surfaces touch, and friction spins each body about its real lever arm. A poke or a grab lands where the pointer ray passes the front-most particle it comes close to (`pick` in `three/jellyPointer.ts`), inside the old bounding sphere, so a long toy is not grabbed by the air beside it and a grab holds the body where the pointer is. Tests: `three/physics.test.ts`, `three/__tests__/jellyPointer.test.ts`
- **Lim is a jelly you can see into.** The body is blended (`limBodyColor` returns its colour and `alpha`): the middle lets most of what is behind through, the edges (the long way through) little, the base more on the lit look, the painted mouth and brows none. Seven bubbles rise inside on their own clocks, found by following the view ray into the body in its rest space, so they ride every squash at their true depth. The body still writes depth, normals and motion, so the screen-space passes treat it as a surface; the ink hull and the eyes draw after it (`renderOrder` 2), so its depth hides the outline's far side and the half of each eye set into the jelly. The lit look's TRAA follows Lim's motion, not the floor seen through it, which shimmers a little at the base while Lim moves fast. Softer than before (stiffness 0.085, damping 0.9), with more of each particle's ripple shown on the skin
- The lit look's grade is bright and cartoonish: a stronger ambient and fill (`DAY` in `lighting.ts`), +0.4 EV, plastics 14% richer than their toon colour, a lighter shadow catcher, and occlusion that marks contacts without greying whole sides (GTAO mixed at 0.3, SSGI's AO at 0.45)
- Eyes counter-turn toward the camera as the head turns (the iris never slides off round the side) and tuck away when facing backwards
- A dev build exposes the scene as `window.__lim` (`__lim.stunt('flip')`) and calls `window.__limFrame(info, [simulate, skin, render])` after each render, for driving and filming beats. `node frontend/scripts/lim-perf.mjs [--graphics high|low] [--throttle 4] [--runs 2] [--toys rocket,duck] [--slow 1]` measures frame times through boot, a burst of new toys, a run of stunts and rest, with the scene's own CPU split per frame; `--slow` lists every frame over 33 ms and every long task by ms into its phase
- Chat history persists in `localStorage` under `lim-chat-v1`; night mode under `lim-chat-night`; sound under `lim-chat-sound`
- I/O stays `invokeDeepseek` (Effect SSE) in `lib/apiService.ts`; the optional `signal` param powers Stop (Esc)
