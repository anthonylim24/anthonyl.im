## Lim's world (items and places)

a visitor can give Lim things and send it places, from the toy box (the brick button in the composer) or by asking in chat. Code: `chat/world/spec.ts` (specs, cleaners for untrusted JSON, the catalog, the model-free matcher), `chat/world/worldApi.ts` (reading the model's orders out of the stream + persistence), `scene/items.ts` (procedural meshes), `scene/fixtures.ts` (the hoop and the goal), `scene/thumbs.ts` (toy-box pictures), `scene/env.ts` (sky backdrop, weather), `scene/scenery.ts` (the landscape behind the stage), `server/src/lim/world.ts` (the model's instructions) and `server/src/lim/model.ts`.
- **One spec format.** An item is 1 to 6 primitive parts (ball, box, cylinder, cone, disc, drop, star, heart, bean, ring) plus traits; a place is a palette, horizon, sun / moon / planet, stars, weather, gravity, grip and a mood; a tweak changes the air (weather, gravity, grip, light) without going anywhere. A part may carry a `paint` (seams, patches, stripes, spots) in an `ink` colour, and a place lists its `scenery` (trees, pines, palms, a volcano, clouds, coral, fish, buildings and so on; `sceneryFor` picks some for a place that names none). Catalog entries (24 items, 8 places) and things the model invents are the same data, drawn by the same gel shader and ink outline. Add catalog entries in `spec.ts` and mirror the id in `server/src/lim/world.ts` (`server/src/lim/world.test.ts` checks parity).
- **Who decides: the chat model.** No separate call decides whether a message is a world order (Jev, below, only picks which Gemini model answers). When the 3D scene is up, the page sends the world (item names, place, weather) with every question to `POST /api/invoke`, whose system prompt gains `worldPrompt()` (`server/src/lim/world.ts`). The model answers normally, or, if and only if the message is an order for the toy world, with one line: `@@world {"ops":[...],"reply":"..."}`. `useChat` watches the stream for that marker, never shows it, and hands the finished line to `onWorld` (`App.tsx`), which cleans and applies the ops and shows Lim's line instead. So a real question is never delayed or misread by a pattern list, and loosely phrased orders ("I want a dragon", "kick it") work. In front of the model sits one shortcut, `localIntent` in `spec.ts`: an order whose whole text parses and names something Lim already knows (catalog, made earlier, or in the world) is acted out at once with no model call. It must stay strict: `chat/__tests__/world.test.ts` holds some 220 strings it may never act on. After editing `worldPrompt()` or changing the model, run `bun scripts/eval-lim-world.ts [model]` (real API, not CI): 127 cases judged through the page's own reader and cleaners: chat messages that must be answered in prose, orders that must come back as `@@world` with the right op, held-out sets of each that appear nowhere in the prompt, a conversation with earlier orders in it, and a page with no 3D scene. It costs real tokens (Carbon is the expensive one). Last full run: Hydrogen 127/127 (November 2026, with the sports rule, part paint and place scenery in the prompt); Carbon 126/127 just before those were added, after the base prompt was widened to general questions (the one miss a 60 s timeout, not a misread). Re-run both before relying on a prompt change. World orders go back to the model as history in their `@@world` form (`ChatMessage.sent`), and toy-box replies the model never wrote are left out. Without a live 3D scene the page sends `world.unavailable` and the model is told the toy box is down. Both the chat and the orders run on Gemini, with Groq as the backup. Before the call, Jev (TypeSafe, `server/src/lim/jev.ts`) asks one Choice question of the message, recent turns and the world: questions go to Gemini 4 Hydrogen, play with the jelly to Gemini 4 Carbon. The route only picks the model; both get the same prompt, so a misroute still answers correctly. No live scene skips Jev (Hydrogen); no key, an error or a 2 s timeout falls back to `limGeminiModel()` (Carbon through December 1, 2026, Hydrogen after); `LIM_GEMINI_MODEL` pins one and skips Jev. With `JEV_API_KEY` set and no model argument, the eval routes each case through Jev and reports routing accuracy too; `--route-only` scores Jev alone with no Gemini calls (cheap: run it after editing `INTENT_QUESTION`). Last route-only runs: 121–122/124, median ~85 ms, no misses in the held-out sets; the misses send toy orders to Hydrogen ("Lim deserves a medal"), which handles them. Carbon takes 5 to 15 s to write an invention, so that wait is a performance: `scene.conjure(true)` gathers a ring of sparkles over a muttering Lim and the reply cycles progress lines (`MAKING` in `App.tsx`); a failed order fizzles and Lim says so. With motion reduced, items simply appear and actions apply their outcome only.
- **Never trust a spec.** Everything from the model or from localStorage passes `cleanItem` / `cleanPlace` / `cleanTweak` / `cleanOps`: enums checked, numbers clamped, part and op counts bounded, names stripped to plain words, colours pulled into the palette, item size normalised. Nothing from the model is used before it is cleaned.
- **What Lim does with things.** Every item: look, kick, hug, put away. By trait: bouncy / floaty → keepy-uppy; edible → eat (hot and cold have their own reactions); hat / glasses → wear (sprung to the head, third poke knocks them off); soft / cozy → nap; musical → dance; scary → flee; prickly → ouch. Lim also plays with items unprompted and reacts to the place's mood. Six items at most; the oldest makes way.
- **Sports.** `hoop` and `goal` are fixtures, not toys (`scene/fixtures.ts`): one of each at most, standing at the right and left edges, outside the six-item limit, built from static colliders (backboard, rim, pole; posts, bar, nets). While one is up the camera pulls back to show the court (`court` in `layout`). A ball that was thrown, kicked or dropped scores when it crosses the rim plane downward or the goal line (`Fixture.scored`): 2 for the hoop, 3 from across the stage, 1 for a goal, credited to whoever sent it last (`toy.by`). The tally shows in a pill on the stage (`LimStage`), is announced to screen readers, and is saved with the world. `shoot` has Lim line up and take three shots (about 6 in 10 go in); "play basketball" / "play soccer" set up whatever is missing (`SPORT` in `spec.ts`). Left alone with a ball and a hoop, Lim practises.
- **Toy box.** Each toy is a card with its real model on top and its name below (`ToyCard.tsx`). `scene/thumbs.ts` owns one small renderer for all of them: a toy is rendered once, copied to the card's 2D canvas and cached, one new still per frame, and only the hovered or focused card animates (one turn, then it settles). Without a GPU the cards show a colour swatch.
- **Landscapes.** `scene/scenery.ts` stands instanced, hand-built props behind the stage where the floor runs out at the backdrop's horizon: one draw call per kind, pop-up-book entrances, wind sway, drifting clouds and fish, lava and smoke, lit windows, distance haze, an ink hull on the near props. Per-vertex and per-instance values are interleaved into one buffer each, because WebGPU allows eight vertex buffers a draw. `SceneryStage.thin()` halves the instances when the stage reports a slow GPU. Check a change with `cd frontend && node scripts/capture-lim.mjs <out-dir>` (court, toy box, every place; needs Vite on :5173).
- The world (items, place, tweak, score) persists in `localStorage` under `lim-world-v1`; things the model made are kept under `lim-made-v1` and listed in the toy box so they can be had again for free.

## Scene and runtime notes

- Theme classes: `chatbot-shadow` (day) / `chatbot-dark` (night), both defined in `frontend/src/chat/chat.css` (`--chat-*` vars), not `index.css`
- `html:has(.chatbot-*)` paints the canvas so iOS safe areas blend
- The 3D scene is lazy (`import('./scene/limScene')`); `LimArt` SVG is the first paint and the no-WebGPU/WebGL fallback
- Reduced motion freezes the jiggle and renders on demand; CSS beats are paused; stunts and easter eggs become a still smile
- Settled toys sleep (no sim, no geometry sync) until something touches them; `Jelly.step` allocates nothing
- Physics is in-house (`three/jelly.ts` + `three/physics.ts`): shape matching in small steps against signed-distance colliders (sphere, capsule, rounded box, torus) with friction and restitution per surface, a rigid core sphere and adaptive substeps (`core`, `maxTravel`) so nothing fast tunnels through a rim or a post, and `bodyImpulse` for body-to-body bounce, rub and spin
- Eyes counter-turn toward the camera as the head turns (the iris never slides off round the side) and tuck away when facing backwards
- A dev build exposes the scene as `window.__lim` (`__lim.stunt('flip')`) and calls `window.__limFrame` after each render, for driving and filming beats
- Chat history persists in `localStorage` under `lim-chat-v1`; night mode under `lim-chat-night`; sound under `lim-chat-sound`
- I/O stays `invokeDeepseek` (Effect SSE) in `lib/apiService.ts`; the optional `signal` param powers Stop (Esc)
