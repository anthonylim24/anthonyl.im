# anthonyl.im — Design Context

Read the section for the route you are changing before any UI work. Always-on engineering rules live in [`AGENTS.md`](AGENTS.md); this file is the only copy of the design context.

Six surfaces share one Vite SPA: Lim the chatbot (`/`), a static home page (`/home`), the lab landing page (`/lab`), **BreathFlow** (`/breathwork`), the **Korea** itinerary (`/trips/korea-2026`) and the generic **trip planner** (`/trips`). Each has its own visual identity; all share the craft principles below.

**Register:** landing + chatbot = brand surfaces; BreathFlow / Korea / Trips = product UIs. Infer the register from the route before applying craft rules.

## House style (every route)

How to design here, step by step, is the [`design-craft`](.agents/skills/design-craft/SKILL.md) skill. The short version:

1. **One living thing, and quiet around it.** Each surface has one centrepiece that responds (Lim, the droplet, the cat, the map) and chrome that stays out of its way. Never two vivid moments in one viewport.
2. **Worlds do not mix.** Each app owns its material, type pair, single accent and motion character. Nothing is shared between them except the standards below.
3. **Type carries the hierarchy.** One display face with character, one text face that disappears; contrast in big steps of size, weight and tracking.
4. **One accent, many tinted neutrals.** The accent means one thing. Dark mode is designed, not inverted.
5. **Motion is physics with a reason.** Springs for anything touched; every animation orients, reveals or confirms. Decorative-only motion is cut.
6. **Every state is designed:** empty, loading, error, long content, phone, dark, reduced motion.

### Boot screens

Each app's first paint is a small scene in its own world, drawn in `index.html` and held until the app can take over (engineering rules in `AGENTS.md`, Routing). It says what is really loading in the app's voice and hands off without a seam.

| App | Scene | Hands off to |
|-----|-------|--------------|
| `/` Lim, and the 404 | LimLoader's sheet, no art | LimLoader |
| `/lab` | A pipette lets a glass droplet fall onto the bench; it ripples and dissolves in ultramarine | The hero's painted still |
| `/breathwork` | Three pigments bloom wet-in-wet under a brush; the wash sits where PaintingLoader's does | PaintingLoader on Home and Session |
| `/trips` | A folded chart opens out, the stops drop on as numbered pins, the compass settles; a trip opened here before is drawn in its own accent | The index or workspace skeletons |
| `/sign-in`, `/sign-up` | An entry stamp comes down on an open passport | Clerk's form |

`/home`, `/privacy` and `/terms` are static pages and need none.

### Standards

- **Contrast:** WCAG AA. 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for control edges and focus rings, in both themes.
- **Reduced motion:** every transform, pulse and camera move honours `prefers-reduced-motion`, keeping the information and dropping the travel.
- **Touch targets:** at least 44 × 44 px.
- **Focus:** a visible ring on every interactive element, in the route's accent.
- **ARIA:** live regions, proper roles and explicit labels on every dynamic surface (the breathing cat, 3D stop pins, status banners).
- **Long strings:** no layout breaks on long Korean / hangul text; user strings use `overflow-wrap: anywhere`.

## Design Context: `/` — Lim, the jelly companion

Code: `frontend/src/chat/` (page shell `frontend/src/App.tsx`). Engineering (files, Lim's world, the model routing and its eval, physics, persistence keys): [`docs/lim.md`](docs/lim.md).

### Users
Visitors who open `/` (from the landing page or a shared link). Recruiters, prospective collaborators, friends, curious engineers. They're trying to get a feel for who Anthony is — fast. They scan, they pivot, they leave if it doesn't earn attention.

### Brand Personality
**Playful, Warm, Crafted.** Lim is a Pixar-soft coral gumdrop of jelly who answers for Anthony. The craft shows in how Lim moves; the chat itself stays calm and readable.

**Emotional goal:** Delight in the first second (Lim pops in, bounces, shakes it off, looks at you), then a reassuring "this person ships" feeling. The mascot is the demo.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** "Sorbet studio": cream day canvas `#FFF6EC`, night-indigo `#1E1833` (toggle in header, or Alt+Shift+S). Pastel blobs, doodles and confetti are set dressing only.
- **Mascot:** the toolkit `Jelly` soft body (shape matching, `upright` righting) in a see-through gelatin shader (`gel` in `limLook.ts`): you see the floor through the jelly and little bubbles rising inside it, a soft toon ramp, light bleeding through the thin far edge, floor bounce, a small studio-window highlight on the crown (masked off the face paint), a swirl on the back of the head so a turn reads as a turn, a smoothed rest-normal blend and an ink hull. Mouth (lens to "o", teeth, tongue), blush and brows are painted in the body shader. Eyes are painted spheres with real lids (`createEyeMaterial`: iris, catch-lights, top / bottom lids meeting at a seam, hearts, dizzy spirals); a shut eye tucks its ink ring away. A springy tuft (`Tuft` in `fx.ts`) gives every move overlapping action and shows the mood (`bend`: wilts when sad or asleep, stands up when startled). A polka-dot floor, a scalloped mint rug, a coral-tinted contact shadow, jelly beans and a bouncy gumball, each with its own seat clear of Lim (three toys on a narrow stage, four on a wide one). Stage edges move a whole body back inside and reflect it; nothing is clipped flat.
- **Graphics:** Auto, High and Low (the sparkles button in the header). High and Auto draw the lit look, which matches the Blender renders: studio light with a real key shadow, clear-coated sorbet plastic, ambient occlusion, floor reflections and one bounce of colour, graded bright and cartoonish (occlusion marks a contact, it never greys a whole side). Low is the toon gel above. Auto keeps phones on the lit look, steps effects down only while frames fall behind, and returns to the toon gel only on a very slow device.
- **Behaviours:** idle breathe + fidgets + blinks + eye darts; typing leans in, nods per keystroke and eyes follow the caret (ALL CAPS startles Lim); send = crouch, hop, squash landing; thinking = wobble + thought bubble; streaming = a new mouth shape per chunk (and a babble syllable with sound on); done = happy squish + confetti and a pirouette (first reply); error = droop + "Try again"; poke = giggle + blush; a gentle drag stretches Lim like taffy and carries it as a teardrop, held facing you (Lim and the toys are drawn through `skinJelly`: the smooth shape-matched goal plus a soft-limited share of each particle's offset, so a hard shake or impact can never crease or facet the surface); a fling bounces off the walls and floor. Every jump is anticipation, stretch, then squash with soft dust discs sliding out along the floor, a floor ripple, a thump that jolts the toys and a little camera shake (`jump` / the landing block in `brain`). Left alone Lim yawns and naps with a nose bubble (sooner at night); shaken or bounced off the walls Lim gets dizzy (spiral eyes, orbiting stars); tap the floor and Lim hops over. Flips are rigid aerial cartwheels and pirouettes are rigid turns (`Jelly.rotate`), so the face stays readable; a stage with no headroom pirouettes instead of flipping.
- **Loading screen:** the first visit to the page opens on a sheet in the page's own colours (cream by day, indigo at night) where the drawn Lim hops along a wobbly jelly progress bar under "Setting the jelly", with a line for each real load step; a poke squishes Lim and gets a word back. "Chat first" drops the sheet at once and a small pill on the stage keeps count until a "Lim's here" cheer. When the scene is ready Lim leaps off the bar and the sheet falls away with a wavy jelly lip as the live Lim pops in. Every beat is transform and opacity only, so it keeps moving while shaders compile; reduced motion gets a still Lim and a plain fade.
- **Easter eggs:** keep them discoverable, never required. Feed Lim a bean (drag it toward the mouth: Lim tracks it, opens wide, snatches it from your hand, chews three times with puffed cheeks, swallows, and the bean's colour rises from the belly for a while; then a burp. Three in a row is a food coma). Ten quick pokes inflate Lim until it pops into six mini Lims that scamper, regroup and merge. A message that names a trick (`stuntFor` in `eggs.ts`: flip, spin, dance, jump, love, sleep, big, tiny, boo, party, pop, a greeting) gets the trick instead of the send hop, and the question is still answered. The Konami code throws a party. The console says hello.
- **Lim's world:** a visitor can give Lim things and send it places, from the toy box (the brick button in the composer) or by asking in chat. Catalog items and things the model invents are the same data, drawn by the same gel shader and ink outline. In the toy box the catalog is shown as Blender renders of sorbet-plastic toys that turn once on hover, and on the stage those same models wobble in the jelly's gel and ink. Lim plays with what it is given (kick, hug, eat, wear, nap, dance, flee), shoots hoops and scores goals, and reacts to a place's mood. Each of the twenty newest catalog toys has a quirk of its own (the rocket lifts off, the music box lulls Lim to sleep, the spinning top makes it dizzy), and every toy answers by its traits: a musical one plays a note, a glowing one lights the floor. Landscapes stand behind the stage like a pop-up book. With motion reduced, items simply appear and actions apply their outcome only. Details: [`docs/lim.md`](docs/lim.md).
- **Sound:** off by default; the header speaker button (or Alt+Shift+M) turns on synthesised foley (`sound.ts`: boings, squeaks, gulp, babble, a dance beat, and the toys' own tunes, squawks and fizzes). No audio files. Lim's voice pitch follows its size.
- **Layout:** desktop stage left, chat panel right. Phones: the header floats over the stage's sky, and the stage takes a share of the screen (half before the first message, less on short phones so the greeting fits; 40% once chatting; compact while typing, when the header slides away). The chat sheet's grip (`role="separator"`) drags or taps the stage bigger, arrow keys too, and the choice is kept under `lim-stage`. Phones get short wording (intro, suggestion pills, placeholder) and keep Copy / Regenerate under the latest reply only.
- **Accent:** Lim coral `#FF7E6B` (send button, Lim's name, list markers). Plum ink `#2B2140` carries the text.
- **Type:** Fredoka display (headings, chips, captions), Inter body.
- **Anti-references:** no purple "AI" gradients, no glowing borders, no rainbow typing indicators. Do not reuse the landing's ultramarine, BreathFlow's watercolour or the Trips atlas.

### Per-route Tokens
- Theme classes: `chatbot-shadow` (day) / `chatbot-dark` (night), both defined in `frontend/src/chat/chat.css` (`--chat-*` vars), not `index.css`
- `html:has(.chatbot-*)` paints the canvas so iOS safe areas blend
- The 3D scene is lazy (`import('./scene/limScene')`); `LimArt` SVG is the first paint and the no-WebGPU/WebGL fallback
- Reduced motion freezes the jiggle and renders on demand; CSS beats are paused; stunts and easter eggs become a still smile

## Design Context: `/breathwork/*` — BreathFlow ("Watercolor breath")

Code: `frontend/src/breathflow/`. Session state is `useSessionEngine` (React state, not Zustand). The centrepiece is a WebGPU soft-body watercolour **cat**: `scene/catScene.ts` (three/webgpu + TSL, lazy-imported by `scene/BloomCanvas.tsx` so three stays out of the initial chunk), positioned by `scene/BloomAnchor.tsx` (painted CSS fallback + brush progress stroke) and driven by `scene/breathDrive.ts` (`useBreathReader` interpolates the 1 Hz engine tick into a smooth amplitude). Built on the shared toolkit in `frontend/src/three/` (`createStage`, `Jelly`, `bindJellyPointer`). `syncJellyGeometry` computes normals + bounding sphere straight on the typed arrays (three's `computeVertexNormals` was half the frame), and `createStage` starts phones at DPR ≤ 1.5 and runs a frame-rate governor that steps the pixel ratio down 15% when the median frame falls behind the display's own rate (60, 120 Hz alike). Pigments live in `pigments.ts`; paper/wash/brush CSS + SVG filters in `styles/watercolor.css` + `components/WatercolorDefs.tsx`. Fonts: Fraunces display (`.bf-display`, SOFT/WONK axes) + Geist body.

### Users
Wellness enthusiasts and people seeking anxiety / stress relief. They open BreathFlow when they need to decompress, build a daily breathing habit, or access structured breathwork techniques backed by science. The context is often evening wind-down, pre-performance calm, or mid-day stress breaks — moments that demand a UI that feels immediately calming upon launch.

### Brand Personality
**Calm, Scientific, Hand-made.** A precision instrument painted by hand: evidence-based protocols, delivered as a living watercolour. Gamification (XP, levels, wax-seal badges) exists to sustain habit, not to entertain.

**Emotional goals:** Immediate calm (wet paint spreading slowly on paper) and quiet confidence (the bloom fills exactly as long as the breath).

### What it does
- **Home:** greeting, streak, week dabs and a daily-goal ring (minutes today against the goal set in Settings); a goal and length chooser that ranks the catalog into one recommended session plus alternatives; "Breathe now · 1 min"; journeys; recent sessions. With a journey under way, wide layouts add a one-line link to its next day under the recommendation.
- **Session:** setup (rounds, cadence, safety check for advanced protocols), then a three-second lead-in ("Settle in", cancellable) before the first breath. A hairline shows whole-session progress with "Round n of N · m:ss left". Esc ends, M mutes, Space pauses. Ending early after the first round keeps the finished rounds as a shorter session (XP scaled to the share completed, never below `MIN_SESSION_XP`); ending in the first round discards it. A session planned shorter than the technique's default earns XP in proportion to its length, never below a quarter (`MIN_LENGTH_SHARE`). A journey day counts only when it is the next day and at least half its rounds were breathed. The session is one pure state machine (`engine/sessionMachine.ts`, driven by `useSessionEngine`); history, XP and the journey step are written exactly once, on the way into the summary. Leaving the page in the background for more than three seconds pauses the session; coming back shows "Welcome back", and Continue runs a three-second lead-in that restarts the current phase from its beginning. While the fullscreen session is up the layout's header, navs and skip link are inert, and focus moves Start → Cancel → Pause → the summary heading. The summary rates calm before and after.
- **Sound** (`engine/audio.ts`, `soundscape.ts`, `voice.ts`): soft bell cues per phase and a triad on completion; an optional synthesised soundscape (tide, rain, drone) whose level and tone follow the breath amplitude; optional spoken cues through the browser's speech synthesis. All off with the master Sound switch. Nothing is downloaded.
- **Journeys** (`journeys/catalog.ts`): a few days of practice in a fixed order, built only from published, non-gated protocols (no new technique or claim). One is active at a time; progress and the daily goal live in `practiceStore` and sync to `user_state.practice`. Finishing one earns the Wayfinder badge. Journey ids are persisted: never rename them.
- **Progress:** the week in a sentence, lifetime figures, level, activity heatmap, an eight-week minutes trend, sessions by time of day, calm shift overall and per technique, hold-time trend, badges, personal bests, history. "Share this week" paints a 1200×630 card (`share/weekCard.ts`) for the system share sheet, or saves a PNG. Every figure is derived from the session log (`gamify/patterns.ts`, `practiceWeek.ts`); nothing extra is stored.
- **Settings:** theme, sound (master, soundscape with preview, spoken cues), haptics, daily goal, data export / import, clear data.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **World:** a hand-painted watercolour on warm cotton cold-press paper (`#F7F1E6`); dark mode is deep ink-blue paper (`#15161C`). A fixed `.bf-paper` tooth overlay multiplies over everything.
- **Cat:** a pen-and-wash cat painted in the technique's pigments. Body and head are `Jelly` soft bodies (the head rides a neck point on the body); ears, paws and a swept-tube tail follow them via `attach`; the face (amber eyes with slit pupils, nose, ω mouth, whiskers, blush) is painted in rest space. It acts out the breath: inhale swells the chest, lifts the head, perks the ears and widens the eyes; a full hold puffs the cheeks and goes still apart from a shiver; the exhale is a "haa" sigh with soft eyes and dropped ears; an empty hold is a contented squint. Idle: blinks, ear twitches, tail swish. One shared light model drives every part: a warm key from the upper left (ragged shadow edge, core shadow, pooling at the turn), a Blinn glint that leaves the paper bare, warm floor bounce in the shadows, and a cool moonlight rim from behind. Day paints it as a Beer–Lambert glaze over the paper; night lays an opaque moonlit body colour over the ink-blue paper with moon-white glints and a dark ink hull (never bare paper, which is dark at night). **High tier** (WebGPU, unless the stage reports it struggling at the lowest pixel ratio; `?quality=base|high` forces a tier): GTAO contact occlusion pooled as pigment where parts meet, a soft bloom on moonlight at night, and pen hatching through the cat's shadow side. **Easter egg:** five taps within 2.5 s pop it (paint splash) into a glossy pink puffball homage (Kirby's face: tall navy eyes fading to blue under a big glint, rosy oval cheeks, a tiny mouth; squeezed "> <" eyes on the inhale and when held; painted over clean paper so the blooms don't show through) that puffs up hugely on the inhale with an open "O", floats with flapping arms on a full hold, and blows air clouds on the exhale; five more taps bring the cat back (the form survives Home → Session). The paper sheet keeps wet-in-wet blooms, a splatter ring that flies out on each inhale, and a shadow wash under the cat. Noise and splatter are baked once in `scene/paintTextures.ts`. Post: paper tooth, colour bleed, vignette, static Three.js Blocks `filmHD` grain (credited on Settings). The session count sits beside the phase word so it never covers the face.
- **Interaction:** poke (a giggle) + drag the cat on Home and session setup, by mouse or touch (a finger on the cat drags it; elsewhere the page scrolls). Grabbed, a body goes loose and stays upright, stretching toward the finger (the cat goes wide-eyed, the puffball squeals and flaps); let go, it boings home, wobbles and squashes on the paper. The contact shadow follows and fades as it lifts. A ball of yarn rests beside the cat on Home and session setup (never during a session): toss, bat or drag it and it bounces off the paper, the cat and the edges of the picture; the cat turns its head to watch and flinches when hit, each exhale nudges the ball away and it rolls back. It shares the cat's light model and casts its own shadow wash; under reduced motion it sits still. During a session, a gentle poke only. Taps count toward the puffball easter egg everywhere. Space toggles pause/resume.
- **Chrome:** matte paper, pill buttons tinted by the active pigment (`--bf-ink`), painted swatches/dabs (`.bf-swatch`, `.bf-dab`), brush strokes (`.bf-brush`). Celebration is a paint-splash burst (`PaintSplash`), not confetti. Badges are wax seals: earned ones are Blender renders of pressed wax in the badge's `sealInk` pigment with its Fraunces monogram (`breathflow/seals/`, [`docs/art.md`](docs/art.md)); unearned ones are a pencilled ring.
- **Anti-references:** ultramarine/SaaS blue-purple, glossy glass chrome, cartoon wellness, cluttered dashboards.

### BreathFlow-specific Principles

1. **Serenity first.** Every design decision should reduce visual noise. White space (paper) is a feature.
2. **Scientific credibility.** Protocols, evidence labels, and safety gating stay first-class; the paint never hides the instructions.
3. **The breath is sacred.** The cat's swell and expressions must track the engine phase exactly (`breathDrive.ts`); surrounding UI fades during session. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the physics freezes (poses come straight from the rest shape) and the scene renders on demand per tick — pacing stays legible through the phase word, count, the cat's pose and face, and the brush stroke.
4. **Habit > novelty.** Gamification exists to drive return visits. Never let the motivational layer overpower the breathwork itself.
5. **WebGPU is progressive.** No GPU → the painted CSS bloom (`.bf-painted-bloom`) carries the same breath via `--bf-amp`. The scene must stay a lazy chunk.

Shared-site Cormorant / parchment does not apply here.

### Users
The same travelers as Korea, plus future trips and the people they share a link with. Phone for in-trip lookups; desktop for planning, AI drafts, and concierge chat.

### Brand Personality
**"The atlas": a cartographer's desk.** Map-first planner. Cool chart-paper surfaces, hairline rules, soft layered shadows, serif names set like map labels, coordinates and data in mono, and one live 3D map per page. Two accents: a constant brand accent (signal orange, `--brand*`) for chrome, and the trip's own accent (`--ta*`) for trip identity; everything else is ink and paper.

**Emotional goal:** See the trip as a place first, then know what happens next.

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Map:** MapLibre GL JS v6 + OpenFreeMap vector tiles (no key). Custom two-palette basemap in `map/mapStyle.ts`: globe projection with atmosphere at low zoom, extruded 3D buildings from zoom ~13.2.
- **Pins and route:** pins are DOM buttons styled by `.atlas-pin*` in `index.css`. Overview map: one numbered pin per day at that day's centroid (click opens the day) over small dots for every stop, no routes. Day map: numbered stop pins plus the route, one shallow arc per leg (deduplicated), a solid 3px trip-accent line on a paper casing with small direction chevrons. Wishlist places: Orchid (`--trips-wish`, wishlist only) hollow heart pins under the stops on the overview and day maps, with a "♥ Wishlist · N" key to hide them; picking one opens its details. A mouse resting on one (120 ms) or keyboard focus on it shows a small card above it, or below or beside it near the map's edge: the category and area in the mono eyebrow, the name in the serif, the street, the first line of the notes, a photo only when its details were already opened this visit (never fetched for the card), and "Click for details" / "Enter for details". It never takes a click, goes on leave, blur, Escape, a press or a camera move, and a tap still just opens the details. Index globe: trip chips, which flip side past the globe's midline. Arrival frames tilt (pitch 52) for city-scale spans. Hovering a list item lifts its pin; clicking a stop pin flies the camera to it and opens a place card ("Stop N of M", Directions, Edit stop, previous / next stepping that flies the camera).
- **Index:** hero + ambient globe (no basemap labels, CSS limb shade, faces the next trip at rest) with one chip per trip. With three or fewer trips the cards sit in one row with no headings; from four they are bucketed Happening now / Upcoming / Past. Each card carries a countdown chip ("In 23 days", "Day 3 of 12", "Ended 2 weeks ago"; none once a trip ended eight weeks ago), and a strip of lifetime figures (trips, days, places, destinations; mono numerals, hairline separators) sits beside "Your trips". "Places" everywhere (cards, figures, the overview tile) counts every place and booking, pinned or not; the overview map chip says how many of them are on the map ("135 of 136 places"). Search `/`, new trip `n`. Card covers are real map stills rendered by one off-screen MapLibre map (`renderStills` in `map/engine.ts`); the SVG chart of the trip's stops (`components/TripCover.tsx`) is the loading / no-WebGL fallback. No stock imagery or photography. The only illustrations are copperplate-style engravings (`Trips/art/`, drawn with ChatGPT Image, [`docs/art.md`](docs/art.md)): compass and dividers on the sign-in gate, a sea serpent on the 404, and nine almanac skies (clear, partly cloudy, cloudy, fog, drizzle, rain, showers, snow, thunder) in the day header. They are alpha masks inked in `--trips-ink-secondary` (`Engraving` in `components/kit.tsx`), so they follow the theme. **Never "Inbox".**
- **Create:** destinations with city autocomplete, a custom date-range calendar (`components/DateRange.tsx`, also used in Trip details; no native date inputs), optional name, "Draft the itinerary with AI" switch with a pace / interest brief (off unless the trip was started from "Draft one with AI", `?ai=1`), sticky submit row, no step circles. The map flies to each destination; on phones it is a small non-interactive strip under the title.
- **Workspace:** `TripWorkspace` renders `/trips/:tripId` (overview) and `/trips/:tripId/day/:dayId` (that day): itinerary pane + sticky map. The full trip header shows on the overview only; day pages use a compact one-row header (trip name links to the overview, Share, ⋯ with "Improve whole trip with AI"); on phones, where that header is hidden, the day's eyebrow leads with the trip name as that link and the day rail ends in a ⋯ ("Share and trip options": Share, then the trip menu). Left / right arrow keys change day. Phones get a docked bottom bar with an Itinerary | Map switch (`radiogroup`) and the concierge button inside it.
- **Overview tabs:** Itinerary · Wishlist · Neighborhoods · Budget · Checklist · Essentials (Radix tabs, underline in ink, `?tab=` in the URL; each panel is a lazy chunk loaded on first open; Checklist shows "3 left"). The Itinerary tab opens with the **now card**: before the trip "Starts in 23 days" with the first booking; during it the trip-accent date tile, the local clock, today's sky in one line ("57° now · Showers 2–5 PM", "Showers until 5 PM" once it has begun), the stop under way, and a Next block (time, serif name, mono countdown ("in 1 h 20"), "Leave by 1:46 PM · 14 min walk", Directions, Open today), then the things a traveller reaches for on the street: Done / Skip / Running late (15 min, 30 min, 1 hour; shifts the rest of the day, with Undo), Route today (the next stops in one maps link), Back to hotel (tonight's stay), Essentials and Log expense; after it a one-line recap and "See the recap". Each day row below carries its forecast at a glance (glyph, the high over the low, the chance of what falls from 30%: "60% showers", "70% snow"), and today's date tile says "Today". Bookings carry the day list's mono outline status chip ("Booked"), never a green pill. Days past the 16-day forecast show the weather typical of the date with a "Typical" label, never as a forecast. The Bookings list reads each one in full: carrier and number, from → to, seat, terminal, "4 nights · Check-out Mon 23 Nov, 11:00"; "Add booking" offers "Paste a confirmation" (AI reads out flights, stays, trains, cars and tables; nothing is added until the traveller picks) and "Enter by hand".
- **Wishlist:** places the traveller might fit in, not yet on a day (search to save one, "Save for later" on any stop). Each place has a category (Food & cafés, Nature & outdoors, Sights & culture, Neighborhoods, Shopping, Bars & nightlife, Experiences, Other), inferred from the place unless the traveller picks one ("Automatic (…)" goes back to inferring). Counted chips filter by category; the list groups by None, Type or Area (the trip's cities in day order, then other towns, Elsewhere, No location), remembered on the device; a filter with nothing left says so with "Show all". A place's name opens its details (`?idea=<id>`, Back closes it): a popover beside the row on wide screens (non-modal: tabbing or clicking away closes it), a sheet on phones, from the map and from a shared link, with live Google photos (several, each credited), the summary, rating and price, hours, address, website, phone and Maps, then the traveller's own notes; without Google it falls back to Wikipedia for landmarks near the point, and never guesses a photo for a business. "Add to day" opens the day picker at once (from the details, Back returns to them) with every day usable except a full one or one that already has the place ("Already on this day"), and a Recommended block above it: up to three days chosen by Gemini 4 Hydrogen from distance to each day's stops, the day's load, its theme and the place's opening days ("Suggested by AI"), or by distance when the model is unavailable, each with one line on why. The pick puts it on that day's route, above anything still to schedule, with an Undo toast. Located wishlist places show on the trip and day maps as Orchid heart pins under the stops. Places scheduled from the wishlist (by the traveller or an AI review) stay listed under "On your days" with a chip that opens their day and "Back to wishlist". Shortening the trip offers to keep the dropped days' stops here.
- **Neighborhoods:** the distinct parts of town the trip spends time in, grouped by AI the first time the tab opens ("Grouped by AI" in brand) and kept until the stops change: at most 12 areas, each stop in at most one, a day trip out of town an area of its own. Each card: a credited Google photo of the area when one can be trusted (none rather than a photo of a landmark that shares its name), the serif name with its local name beside it ("Hannam & Itaewon 한남동 & 이태원동"), a mono "Days 2, 9 · 7 stops" line (the city first when the trip has several), what the area is like in a sentence or two, "Show on map" (pressed while drawn) and "Stops by day", the area's stops under their days, each linking to that stop. Show on map draws one area at a time under the pins in the trip accent and frames it top-down: OpenStreetMap's outline (a hairline) when it holds most of the area's stops, otherwise a soft shape around the stops with a dashed edge, never passed off as a boundary. A chip on the map names the area, says "Outline from OpenStreetMap" or "Drawn around your stops", and clears it; on phones the dock switches to Map. Loading shows card skeletons and says the first time takes about half a minute; a failure is one calm line with Try again; a trip with no located stops says the tab fills as places are added.
- **Day:** a List | Timeline switch (remembered on the device). Timeline is an hour ruler with stops as blocks by minute, overlaps in lanes, "Overlaps X" / "Tight" badges, labelled free time and an Anytime list; each block and Anytime row has an edit pencil ("Edit Gion", shown on hover or focus where there is a pointer, always on touch) that opens the stop's editor and leaves the layout as it was; legs in both views are honest estimates with a mode icon ("1.8 km · ~15 min by transit", none out of a flight or train booking); in the list, the leg between two stops that overlap says "No time to travel". Today gets a "Now" hairline in the trip accent, and opens at the top with the stops already over folded into one quiet row ("4 stops done · 8:00 AM–1:30 PM", Show / Hide, shared by both layouts; a stop in it that is selected or deep-linked opens it). Legs sit above the stop they lead into, so Now comes before the walk ahead, and between timed stops they say what is free and when to leave ("940 m · 12 min walk · 1 h 08 free", "Leave by 3:18 PM"). A clash names the other stop by its map number ("Overlaps ②"). The day header carries the day's sky as a band under the title at every width: the engraving for that weather, the high set large in the serif over the low, the sky in words with the chance of rain when it is 30% or more, and sunrise / sunset. It is a live Open-Meteo forecast for the next 16 days (eyebrow "Forecast", Fahrenheit for US locales), the AI's weather further out ("Estimate · from the plan", set with "≈"), "Not available right now" with "Try again" when the forecast does not come, and nothing when neither is known; its place is held while a forecast loads. The timeline reads the same forecast hour by hour: temperatures under every third hour, a hatched rain band beside the hours with its chance (never over a stop), a rain line on the stops it meets (in ink when it is 70% or a storm), and sunrise and sunset as hairlines under the stops, named in the summary. The day sky gives the wettest window and, on its own line, the stop it falls on and when ("Showers 2–5 PM · 60% chance", "Falls on ⑤ Gion and Hanami-koji · 3:30–5:00 PM"; the stop finds itself on a tap), with "Rework around the showers" beside it in both layouts, which opens Ideas briefed with the wet window and indoor places. Free time is counted after the journey and says when to leave. List cards carry the same overlap and rain lines, and list legs the same wet glyph as the timeline's; from 70% or a storm the rain line is set in ink. On dark the engraving is printed on a lamp-lit oval of paper, so a clear sun never reads as a black one. The untimed stops after the last timed one sit under "Anytime" with no legs (an untimed stop between timed ones keeps its place: the list is the route), and on today the stops already over go quiet, as on the timeline. On today, free time counts from now. An empty day says "Nothing planned yet" once. Under the title: "Sleeping at Memmo Alfama · night 3 of 4" (stays with a check-out date cover every night up to it), and on check-out day when to leave. A public holiday where the day is spent gets a quiet line in the day sky ("Christmas Day · public holiday: shops and museums may close or keep short hours"). Once a day has come, each stop has quick Done / Skip / Loved it and the day has its journal; skipped stops leave the route and its numbers but stay on the map as faded dots. A stop starting in the small hours after an evening one belongs to that night everywhere (sorting, countdowns, the timeline, calendar export). A day can be lived in its own time zone (multi-country trips).
- **Money:** every stop can carry a cost (`CostField`, in the trip's currency by default). The Budget tab: serif budget figure with a hairline meter (spent in ink, planned hatched, "€420 left" / "€80 over" in mono), by-category bars, the expense log grouped by date (inline add / edit, paid by, split between), "Planned from your stops", who's travelling, and Settle up ("Sam pays Anthony ₩42,000"). Other currencies convert at live Frankfurter rates; one that cannot convert is shown as "not in totals", never guessed.
- **Prep:** the Checklist tab has Packing / Before you go / Documents, inline edit, assignees once two people travel, "Suggest from your plan" (adapters for the destination's plugs, visa check, bookings to print…; never duplicates) and Clear checked with Undo. Once the trip has ended the pre-departure suggestions go, and an empty list asks what is still to do now you're back. Essentials opens with "Show to a driver": tonight's stay in large type with its address and directions. Then per country: local time and the difference from the viewer, currency with a two-way converter at today's rate (home currency from the viewer's zone, changeable), plugs and mains, emergency numbers as 44px tap-to-call buttons, driving side, tipping, language, calling code, and the public holidays during the trip.
- **Recap:** from the trip menu, or the after-trip card. Days, places visited, cities, distance ("≈ 8.2 km", straight lines between visited stops), loved stops, journal excerpts and spend by category; "Share as a picture" draws a 1200×630 card (the trip's chart, the figures, a long name on two lines) for the share sheet or as a PNG; Print makes its own paper copy.
- **Offline and saving:** one save line (Saved / Saving / "Offline · will save when back online" / "Not saved · reason · Retry"), in the header on wide screens and in the bottom bar on phones. Edits made offline are kept on the device and sent when the connection comes back; the last trips opened read offline as a labelled copy while the browser is still signed in as their owner, with nothing that needs the server on offer. Enter that confirms a Korean or Japanese word never submits a field.
- **Keyboard:** ⌘K / Ctrl+K opens a command palette from anywhere (a "Search ⌘K" button in the header too): actions (share, AI, details, calendar, print, copy, Instagram, new trip), Go to (overview, previous / next day, the overview tabs), every day and stop, every trip, the theme and the look (atlas or Liquid Glass). Matching ignores case and accents and handles Hangul as it is typed. `?` opens a shortcuts sheet.
- **Print and copy:** "Print itinerary" lays the trip out as paper (cover with the trip map, a page per day with its map still and numbered pins matching the list, a bookings appendix; ink on white whatever the theme; the PDF is named after the trip). "Copy itinerary" puts a plain-text version on the clipboard for a chat or an email, with a short "Itinerary copied" in the dock.
- **Editing:** inline trip / day titles. Stop cards have a time column on the left (above the title on phones); the drag grip (Motion `Reorder`) shows on hover / focus and is hidden on phones; Alt+↑ / ↓ reorders with a live announcement, and the item editor keeps Move up / Move down. The day menu offers "Sort stops by time" when times are out of order. Stop times use a typed `TimeField` (`parseClock` in `meta.ts`) with "Add end time". One "Add a place, note or link…" composer per day (its accessible name is the same words) with keyless place search (picking a result pins it on the map immediately; Enter takes the top result only when it is near the trip and named what was typed (Hangul is read against the English name, 경복궁 as Gyeongbokgung Palace), otherwise it adds the text as typed, and places far from the trip are listed only when their name matches); one stop editor, opened from the card, a timeline block's pencil, the map's place card ("Edit stop"), the budget or a link: under the card in the list on a wide screen, otherwise a sheet titled with the stop (a dialog on a wide screen, a bottom sheet on a phone) with Delete, Move up / down, Duplicate, Move to another day, Maps and Done in its foot, 44px apart. It asks only for what a stop needs (`workspace/stopFields.ts`): a place its name, time, location and notes; a booking its type, status and times; a note its words; the rest wait behind "Add status" / "Add cost" / "Add link" / "More booking details" chips until they hold something. A booking has one Status (Booked, Tentative, Pending, Done, Skipped; `bookings/status.ts`), in the same words as its card's chip. An untimed place after a timed one offers "Start at 2:35 PM" and says why ("after Galleria (about 2 h there), 12 min walk"; `timeline/suggestStart.ts`), never past midnight or where it would not fit before the next stop. A stop moved to another day keeps its time and lands where that falls; the stop's ⋯ menu also has "Copy to other days…", a sheet of day checkboxes ("Every day" / "Clear", "Copy to 3 days") whose copies keep the stop's time and land where it falls on each day; Undo / Redo for the last 30 changes (Cmd/Ctrl+Z, Shift for Redo) with an Undo toast after destructive ones (delete, clear day, move to another day, copy to other days, sort, Instagram import, shortened dates, a replaced itinerary) that stays 12 s and pauses on hover or focus; autosave with the save line above, merging with the concierge's and collaborators' changes and asks Keep mine / Use theirs only on a true clash; AI runs show a Stop button, which cancels a review on the server and says how it ended (stopped with nothing changed, finished first with its stops landed, or not confirmed); trip details dialog (name, destinations, dates, colour accent, description, status, time zone); duplicate trip, delete trip (owner), clear day.
- **Icons:** sparkle = AI generation, lightbulb = "Ideas for this day", chat glyph = concierge.
- **Instagram import:** "Places from Instagram" sheet. Paste a post or Reel link (pasting is the whole gesture; the day composer recognises links too). Posts are read quickly first (caption + comments) and re-read with the video on their own if that finds nothing; a hairline progress bar and plain step labels show the read. Each place found gets a row (name, address, the line from the post) and a pin on the sheet's own map; "+ Day N" adds it to the day in view or the nearest day, "Add all" adds a post's places at once; untimed imports land under a "To schedule" heading and stay off the route and numbering; and places already on the trip show "On day N". Everything found is kept under Saved for every trip (search, remove). Never show pipeline logs or raw errors.
- **Sharing:** link-first. Owner picks "Can view" / "Can edit" and copies an invite link; anyone signed in who opens `/trips/join/:token` joins with that role. Owner changes roles, removes people, turns links off; collaborators can leave.
- **Concierge:** a chat that acts on the trip. It also knows the budget, what was spent, who is travelling and the open to-dos. Answers lead with the pick; a live status line shows real tool activity ("Checking Google Maps", "Searching the web"); place cards carry a photo, rating, hours for that day, the walk from the plan and "Add to Sat 14 Nov · 13:00"; changes arrive as one proposal card the traveller ticks, applies and can undo; 2 to 3 follow-up chips after each answer; the conversation is kept per trip (`trips-concierge-v1:<tripId>`) with New chat; location is shared for one message only, on request.
- **AI:** full-trip draft (`/generate`; from create, the empty overview, or "Start a fresh draft" with a replace confirmation), per-day "Ideas for this day" and whole-trip "Improve with AI" (`/enhance`; added places are auto-applied, flashed in the list and dropped onto the map; other suggestions are reviewed from a checklist, including moving a stop to another day and swapping two days; every day a review touches ends in clock order; Apply opens "What changed", a ribbon per day that moves from the old plan to the new plus a row per change, and names anything it couldn't apply with the reason, leaving it in the review to try again), concierge chat FAB (`TripChat`, `chat.stylex.ts`). When a hotel is added or changed, the workspace offers "Staying at X. Rework the plan around it?" (never automatic); yes runs a whole-trip review briefed on the new base. A draft that could not fill every day says which days need another go.

The **trip** accent (`--ta`, `--ta-fill`, `--ta-on`, `--ta-soft`, `--ta-ring`; set by `data-trip-accent`, defaults to the brand) is reserved for trip identity: pins, route, date tiles, the status eyebrow, the pinned-location row, covers. Trip accents: rose "Poppy" `#e5484d`, amber "Saffron" `#f2a71b` (dark ink on fill), emerald "Jade" `#0f9b76`, sky "Cobalt" `#2f7bf0`, violet "Iris" `#7c5cff`. Primary buttons are ink.

- **Anti-references:** the previous toy / sticker look, glassmorphism stacks, gradient text, stock travel photography, generic SaaS dashboards. Do not restyle Trips into the Korea dossier. The one sanctioned exception is the opt-in Liquid Glass look below.

### The Liquid Glass look (opt-in)

A second look the traveller chooses (the droplet button in the header, or "Use the Liquid Glass look" in ⌘K); the atlas stays the default and nothing changes for anyone who does not pick it. It is kept per device (`trips-look`, `<html data-trips-look="glass">`), independent of light / dark. It is Apple's iOS 26 Liquid Glass, not generic glassmorphism: one layer of glass over content, never glass over glass.

- **Material:** floating chrome is glass that bends what is behind it. In Chromium each surface gets its own displacement lens (a convex squircle bezel: what is behind the rim shows nearly sharp and visibly bent, the middle stays frosted; `glass/refraction.ts`, at most twelve at once, on-screen only); everywhere else (Safari, iOS, Firefox) the same surfaces are frosted (blur, saturation, a lift in brightness), which is the designed look on the phone, not a fallback. A hairline specular rim catches the light, which follows a hovering pointer on desktop and rests at the top left. Cards, rows and fields are tinted only, so scrolling costs nothing extra.
- **Wallpaper:** soft pools of the trip's accent with two companion hues (sky and lilac beside a warm accent, peach and rose beside a cool one), painted once by a small WebGL2 shader and kept as a still; a CSS gradient until it arrives and wherever WebGL2 is missing. It never drifts (idle means no frames).
- **Shape:** the header, the day rail and the phone dock are floating capsules inset from the edges (the dock respects the safe area and carries the concierge button in its right slot); segmented controls and the overview tabs are capsule tracks with a glass thumb; sheets and dialogs take 2rem corners over a light scrim with no blur of its own; the phone map runs under the dock.
- **Type:** the system face (SF Pro via `-apple-system`), bold large titles, the eyebrows in SF with tracking; Instrument Serif and Fragment Mono belong to the atlas.
- **Colour:** both accents keep their jobs. The concierge button is brand-tinted glass. On the wallpaper the text tones deepen one step (`--ta` takes `--ta-strong`, `--brand` `#9a3409`, tertiary ink `#4f545b`) so everything clears 4.5:1.
- **Contrast floor:** the wallpaper is held in a luminance band (≥ 0.72 light, ≤ 0.028 dark; `glass/palette.ts`, tested against every ink it carries), and each material's tint is at least what keeps its text at 4.5:1 over a busy, blurred backdrop.
- **Where it is off:** print, the recap card (and its picture) and the photoreal 3D view keep the atlas as they are (`.trips-glass-off` on the photoreal host); no backdrop filter ever sits over the photoreal canvas.
- **Preferences:** reduce transparency makes every surface solid and drops the wallpaper; increase contrast makes it solid with every edge drawn; reduced motion freezes the light and removes the press squish (a spring `scale` on buttons, links, tabs and segments).
- **Cost:** the glass code is a lazy chunk loaded only in this look; `glass.css` is scoped to `[data-trips-look="glass"]` and inert in the atlas.

### Trips-specific Principles

1. **One map per viewport.** Keep the engine a lazy chunk (`maplibre`).
2. **AI-added places must carry structured locations**, never prose only.
3. **Never add destination-specific routes.**
4. **44px targets; draw your own focus ring.** Trips sets `.trips :focus-visible { outline: none }`, so every new control needs a `--brand-ring` ring (`a.focusable`).
5. **Editable titles** use the `trips-title-input` / `trips-day-title-input` classes to opt out of the 16px phone input pin.
6. **Portals** go through `kit.tsx` `Modal`, which re-declares the token scope (`.trips.trips-portal`).
7. **The photoreal 3D view must unmount** when closed (never React `Activity`).
8. **Brand for chrome, trip accent for identity.** Never tint focus rings, AI actions, switches or links with `--ta*`.
9. **One state, one owner.** The workspace remounts per trip (`key={tripId}`); day-scoped overlays reset when the day changes; async results (AI runs, chat writes, duplicates) are dropped once their trip or component is gone; editing is locked while an AI run is in flight; concierge writes to the itinerary are serialised. Model a mode as one union (`status`, `mutation`, `action`), not several booleans.
